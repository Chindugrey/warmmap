# Who's in Your Corner: Product Document

Nov 13, 2026 · @arjun

Everyone has a few people in their corner. This product helps you find them, tell them, and earn a seat at a small, exclusive table with the people who vouch back.

## Why this product

The best things in a founder's life rarely come from a cold message. They come from someone who says "I know them, let me introduce you." We built this to make that feeling visible, shareable and a little bit fun.

**What people feel today**

- Everyone has a handful of people who'd pick up at 2 a.m., believed in them early, or made one intro that changed their year. Most of those people have never been told.
- Networking feels like work: badges, pitches, cards nobody keeps.
- Inboxes are full of messages written by machines. A real "I'd vouch for you" from a real person has never meant more.

**How we got here**

1. We build Zeyhi, which helps founders reach customers through people who know them instead of cold email. Every week we saw that one vouch from the right person beat a hundred messages.
2. We tried a poker-table toy, then a map of the cities where your people live. Both were fun, but they were about bragging.
3. Ten imagined expert reviewers then put the idea through 100 hard questions. That made it safe and accountable, but serious and slow.
4. The breakthrough was simple: it shouldn't be about how many people you know. It should be about **who is in your corner**, and about telling them so.

**The insight:** being named as someone's "2 a.m. person" feels wonderful. That feeling is what gets shared, and every share brings in a new player.

## The product on one page

A two-minute game that shows you who's in your corner, a message that tells them, and a small exclusive table for the people they vouch for.

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| The game | Tap a glowing globe, answer 12 warm questions with a name and city, and watch your people light up across the map. No login. |
| The reveal | Four swipeable cards: how many people, how many cities, your "2 a.m. person", and your type (The Glue, The Quiet Connector, The Door Opener). |
| The vouch-back | A message you send each person yourself: "You were my answer to..." They tap once to vouch for you, then find their own corner. |
| The Table | One dinner a month per city, 14 seats: 6 super connectors, 6 of the most-vouched people, 2 newcomers. Pure games, no pitching. |
| The receipt | Every table prints a receipt of doors opened, and 30 days later counts the meetings that actually happened. |

**The 12 questions**

1. Who'd pick up your call at 2 a.m.?
2. Who believed in you before it made sense?
3. Which customer became a friend?
4. Who's your customer champion?
5. The boss you'd work for again?
6. The colleague who covered for you?
7. Who always says "let me introduce you"?
8. The mentor or investor who actually texts back?
9. Who do you call first when you win?
10. The friend in another city you miss most?
11. Who made this year better?
12. Who do you want to meet this year?

**Name:** working name "Who's in your corner?", played on Warm Table. Trademark check before launch; "Say Hi" stays out of the name.

**Cities:** Bengaluru, then Hyderabad, then Delhi. One city abroad later, hosted by a member who lives there.

## User flow

The whole product is one loop: you find your people, you tell them, and every vouch-back can bring a new player and move you closer to a seat.

&#91;embedded content: User flow · one loop, one path to the Table\]

1. **Tap the globe (5 s).** One glowing globe, one button: "Find my people".
2. **12 questions (90 s).** Name and city, company optional. Each answer lights up a city with a small reaction ("Good one.").
3. **See your corner (20 s).** Four cards: people and cities, your 2 a.m. person, your type, and a share card.
4. **Send vouch-backs (30 s each).** A pre-written WhatsApp message you edit and send yourself: "You were my answer to..."
5. **They vouch back (10 s).** One tap and one line about you. Then: "Now find who's in your corner."
6. **They play too.** The loop starts again with a new player.
7. **Get a seat.** The most vouched (different people only, counted up to 5) get "Your people got you a seat."

## The Table: how an evening runs

One long dinner, 14 people, about 2.5 hours, pure games. Nobody pitches, and doors open anyway.

**Before**

1. **Two weeks out:** the host invites 6 super connectors personally. The 6 most-vouched people and 2 newcomers get "Your people got you a seat."
2. **One week out:** each guest picks two dots: what they're looking for (co-founder, a hire, an investor, customers, a partner, advice, a new city) and what they can help with.
3. **48 hours out:** anyone who can't come releases their seat, and it goes to the next person on the list.

**The evening**

| Time | Game | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7:00 | Hello Bingo | Find someone for each square: "has hired a CTO", "was someone's 2 a.m. person", "looking for a co-founder" |
| 7:30 | Introduce your neighbour | Two minutes to learn about the person beside you, then you introduce them |
| 7:50 | Two truths and an ask | Two true facts and one ask, said out loud |
| 8:10 | Pass the Door | Each ask goes round on a card. Anyone who can open the door stamps it, and the little printer prints a slip. |
| 8:40 | Who's your hello? | The host calls out a city or a need. First real name offered wins a chip. |
| 8:55 | The Mystery Seat | One guest was a surprise. Revealed at dessert, they answer three asks. |
| 9:10 | The receipt | The printer strip is pulled out and everyone is in the photo |

House rules: pitch and you buy dessert; phones in a bowl until the receipt prints.

**After**

1. **Next morning:** each guest gets their stamped door card and a thank-you prompt to post if they want.
2. **Day 14 and day 30:** a one-tap check for each stamp: met, not yet, or no.
3. **Day 30:** the Room Receipt goes public with counts only: doors stamped, coffees, interviews, meetings held.

## Virality and success metrics

It's working when each player brings in at least half a new player through vouch-backs, and when tables turn into real meetings. All targets are our own estimates, to be replaced by real numbers after Bengaluru.

**The viral loop in one line:** new players per player = vouch-back messages sent × share who vouch back × share of those who then play.

Example at target: 4 messages × 50% vouch back × 30% play = 0.6 new players per player, before any posts or shares.

| Stage | Metric | Target (first 30 days) | Rethink if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrive | Visitors who tap the globe | 60% | Under 40% |
| Play | Players who finish at least 5 questions | 60% | Under 40% |
| Reveal | Players who share the corner card anywhere | 20% | Under 10% |
| Vouch-back | Messages sent per player | 4 | Under 2 |
| Vouch-back | Recipients who vouch back | 50% | Under 25% |
| Loop | Vouchers who then play their own game | 30% | Under 15% |
| Loop | New players per player | 0.6 | Under 0.3 |
| Table | Seats filled (of 14) | 13 | Under 10 |
| Table | Doors stamped per table | 20 | Under 10 |
| Business | Stamps that become real meetings within 30 days | 40% | Under 25% |
| Business | Zeyhi pilot conversations started by the 90-day mark | 3 | 0 |

**Scale goals (estimates):** 1,000 players and 400 vouch-backs in Bengaluru by the first table; 5,000 players across three cities by day 90.

**What we never measure:** likes, followers, or how many people someone knows. The numbers that matter are vouch-backs and meetings held.

## Competitors and how we compare

Nobody else starts from "who trusts you": event tools sell seats, dinner clubs seat strangers, and AI connectors match profiles. We seat people who were vouched for, and we count the meetings that follow.

| Alternative | What they do well | Where they stop | How we're different |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Timeleft](https://timfrin.substack.com/p/inside-timelefts-journey-to-connecting) | Dinners with strangers in hundreds of cities; reported €18M annual revenue in 2025 | Seats strangers for company, not for a purpose | Every guest was vouched for, and every ask can turn into a door |
| [Boardy](https://www.upstartsmedia.com/p/would-you-trust-ai-to-help-raise) | An AI that talks to you and proposes double opt-in intros | A machine decides the match; founder reviews are mixed | A person who knows you vouches, and you see who |
| [Lunchclub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunchclub) | Algorithm-matched 1:1 video meetings | Growth stalled and the team pivoted away | Groups at a real table, built on existing trust |
| [Bumble Bizz](https://bumble.com/en-in/help/bumble-bizz%EB%A5%BC-%EC%97%AC%EC%A0%84%ED%9E%88-%EC%82%AC%EC%9A%A9%ED%95%A0-%EC%88%98-%EC%9E%88%EB%82%98%EC%9A%94) | Swipe to meet professionals | No longer available; swiping didn't build trust | No swiping. Your people bring you in. |
| [Hampton](https://www.inc.com/cara-cannella/inside-hampton-sam-parrs-bootstrapped-solution-to-the-founder-loneliness-epidemic/91284896) | Vetted founder groups; moved in-person because members wanted proximity | About $15,000 a year and roughly 4% accepted | Free to play; your seat is earned through vouches, not paid for |
| Luma, Meetup, Partiful | Easy event pages and RSVPs | Anyone can register; no trust, no follow-up | The guest list is the product, and the receipt is the follow-up |
| Supper clubs | A lovely evening | A good night, then nothing | Doors stamped tonight, meetings counted at 30 days |
| LinkedIn | Everyone's network in one place | Connections without accountability or warmth | A vouch with someone's name on it |
| WhatsApp groups | Where people already talk | No memory and no structure | Paths, seats and receipts |

**Why we can win**

- **Emotion first.** Being named someone's 2 a.m. person is a feeling no event page or algorithm gives you.
- **The invite is a compliment.** Others ask for sign-ups. We send "you were my answer to..."
- **Exclusive but fair.** You can't buy a seat. Your people earn it for you.
- **Proof.** The 30-day receipt shows whether doors actually opened.

## Rules we keep and risks

Six rules keep it warm, safe and fair; three risks are worth watching from day one.

**Rules**

1. **Names stay on your phone.** A name is saved only when that person vouches back themselves.
2. **Nothing is sent for anyone.** Every message is sent by a person, by hand.
3. **No popularity contest.** Only vouches from different people count, the count stops at 5, and nobody's count is shown publicly.
4. **Protect super connectors.** Each person sees at most 10 vouch requests a month, each with a "maybe later" button.
5. **No paid seats, no lottery, no prizes.** Seats go to super connectors, the most vouched and newcomers. Nobody is paid for an intro.
6. **Safe tables.** Code of conduct at every RSVP, two hosts, at least a third women, venues only, one-tap private report.

**Risks**

| Risk | Early sign | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The fun never turns into business | Few stamps become meetings by day 30 | Lean on the customer-champion and want-to-meet questions; seat by fit |
| Vouch-back messages feel spammy | Fewer than 1 in 4 vouch back | Make the message more personal; cap at 5 messages per player |
| Interest fades after launch | New players drop week over week | New moments every month: city openings, table receipts, a year-end "corner recap" |
