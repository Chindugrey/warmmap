Chapter one

It always came through someone

Think back for a moment. Your first job. The flat you live in. The doctor you trust. The co-founder, the first customer, the person who said yes when nobody else would. Almost none of it began with a stranger's message. It began with someone who said, quietly, "I know someone."

Chapter two

Now everyone can reach everyone

Inboxes overflow. Feeds never sleep. Every message seems to be selling something, and every one of us has become someone's target. We have never had more ways to talk, or fewer people who'll pick up.

Chapter three

But warmth still travels by hand

A few people would answer your call at two in the morning. They live in different cities, sometimes different countries. Together they're your corner. Light them up one lantern at a time, then send the map to them. When a friend finds their own lantern on it, they'll smile, vouch for you in one tap, and make a map of their own.

      Chapter four

      The table

      Once a month, in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Delhi, fourteen people sit down to one long dinner. You can't buy a seat, and you can't apply. The friends who vouch for you bring you in.

      6 super connectors, invited by hand
      6 people their friends vouched for most
      2 newcomers

      No pitching. Pitch, and you buy dessert. Phones sleep in a basket until the end.

      How the evening goes

      1. 7:00Hello bingo. Find the person who was someone's 2 a.m. call.
      2. 7:30Introduce your neighbour. You speak for them; they speak for you.
      3. 7:50Two truths and an ask. Said out loud, kept small.
      4. 8:10Pass the door. Your ask travels round the table on a card. Whoever can open it stamps it, and a little printer prints the slip.
      5. 8:40Who's your hello? A city is called. The first real name wins.
      6. 8:55The mystery seat. One guest was a surprise all along.
      7. 9:10The receipt. Everyone in the photo, holding one long strip of paper.

      Chapter five

      And it was written down

      Every door that opens at the table is printed that night. Thirty days later we print a second slip: the coffees, the interviews, the meetings that actually happened. We count doors, never likes.
